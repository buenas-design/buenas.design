No, we won't design exactly what you want

And that's good news. A brand designer's job isn't to follow your taste, it's to guide your brand somewhere better. Here's how that works in practice.

By Julia Sonda · November 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Let's start with a confession: we're not going to design exactly what you want. At least, not exactly.

I know, I know. You might be thinking: but I'm the one paying! Shouldn't the designer do what I ask? Hmmm, it's not quite like that. Especially when we're talking about brand design.

Your brand isn't about your taste

Your business is like your baby. You built it, you care for it, and you have strong opinions about it. That's completely normal, and honestly, it's beautiful. But sometimes your personal preferences and what's best for the business point in different directions.

You might love minimal black and white, while your clients are families looking for warmth and fun. You might be tired of a color you see every single day, while your audience is only just starting to recognize you by it.

A brand isn't made to please its owner. It's made to connect with the people you want to reach.

So what are you paying for?

Not just hours and files. You're paying for the opinion and guidance of people who spend every day thinking about how brands are perceived. Our job is to take you by the hand, help you notice your preferences, and find a direction built on strategy, values, personality and audience. That's where really good design happens.

Strategy beats trends. Strategy beats personal taste. Every time.

What guiding looks like in practice

Guiding doesn't mean ignoring you. It means your opinion comes into the process at the right moments, with the right questions:

  • Before we design, we ask about your audience, your values and where you want to go, not only which colors you like.
  • When we present concepts, we explain the thinking behind each one, so you can judge them by what they do and not only by how they feel at first glance.
  • When you give feedback, we help you tell the difference between “this doesn't work for my clients” and “this isn't my favorite”. Both are valid. Only one should change the design.

(And if you really hate yellow, we won't use yellow. Relax! haha)

Why letting go a little pays off

The clients who get the most out of a brand project are the ones who arrive curious, not with the final answer already in mind. They share references as inspiration, not as orders. They trust the process enough to see something unexpected and ask why before saying no.

That's when a brand stops being a reflection of one person's taste and becomes something bigger: a visual universe your clients recognize, remember and choose.

Before you hire a brand designer

Ask yourself one question. Do I want someone to execute my idea, or someone to help me find the best one? Both exist, and both have their place. But if you want a brand that's strategic, cohesive and truly yours, look for a guide.

We'd love to be yours.
